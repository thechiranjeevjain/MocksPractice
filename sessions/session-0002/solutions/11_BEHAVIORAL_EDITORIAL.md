# Round 11 Editorial — Reliability Versus Deadline

## TRIGGER

A leadership prompt asks for judgment under delivery pressure and evidence of influence without formal authority.

## PATTERN

STAR-L: situation, task/authority, action with options/trade-offs, result with evidence, learning.

## INVARIANT

The story must preserve factual ownership and make the candidate's decision/action distinguishable from the team's work.

## TEMPLATE

`Deadline/risk -> my responsibility -> evidence -> options -> recommendation/influence -> guardrail/action -> result -> learning.`

## FALLBACK

“We worked hard and delivered” omits the decision, conflict, individual contribution, and measurable result.

## OPTIMIZATION

Spend most time on actions and reasoning. Use one concrete metric or observable outcome, not a list of generic virtues.

## Final answer guide

Use a real example. State the business consequence of delay and the specific correctness/reliability risk. Define your authority. Explain the evidence gathered—tests, incident history, load result, error budget, or dependency behavior. Present at least two options and the trade-off you recommended. Describe how you communicated risk and influenced dissenters. Name the minimum guardrail: feature flag, canary, rollback, reconciliation, rate cap, monitoring, or scope reduction. Close with measurable outcome, remaining downside, and a specific behavior changed afterward.

If the date cannot move, protect the irreversible/correctness boundary, ship the smallest reversible slice, instrument it, canary it, and explicitly defer nonessential scope.

## Complexity

Aim for 2-3 minutes before follow-ups.

## Edge cases

The result was mixed; leadership rejected the recommendation; the candidate had limited authority; or no numerical metric exists. Be honest and use observable qualitative evidence.

## Observed candidate mistakes

None in this sample. Red flags include invented ownership, blaming others, and no learning.

## Next variation

Retell the same event from the perspective of a stakeholder who disagreed with you.
